# Manuscript figure and table legends

## Shared definitions

These figures use the frozen public-vignette benchmark, not prospectively observed clinical outcomes. “Agreement” means a valid predicted grade belongs to the source-derived acceptable-label set. Grade order is A < B < C < D, with D the most urgent. Undertriage and overtriage mean predictions below the lowest or above the highest acceptable grade, respectively. Thus C is acceptable for C/D but constitutes undertriage for a D-only reference. Technical failures are retained separately and remain in planned denominators. Variants and repeated requests are not independent patients. “Clear” and “ambiguous” refer to single-label and acceptable-set source references, respectively. Only the prespecified first-round clear-label JEV–Luna comparison is the primary analysis. Other displays are descriptive, sensitivity or exploratory analyses unless stated otherwise. Figure implementation and historical rescoring were undertaken after data collection.

Model abbreviations identify the frozen endpoints: JEV, `typesafe/jev-1.13` (catalogue version `typesafe/jev-1.13-20260917`); Luna, `openai/gpt-5.6-luna` (catalogue version `openai/gpt-5.6-luna-20260709`, reasoning disabled); Gemini, `google/gemini-3.1-flash-lite` (catalogue version `google/gemini-3.1-flash-lite-20260507`, minimal reasoning); DeepSeek, `deepseek-flash` (documented as DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, thinking disabled). The first three use their frozen OpenRouter routes; DeepSeek uses its direct API. These identifiers do not establish equivalence to any consumer product.

## Figure 1 | Reference-label agreement, error direction and repeated decision consistency

**a,** First-round agreement among 480 clear-label inputs from 15 base scenarios, all 960 main inputs from 30 scenarios, and 480 ambiguous-label inputs from 15 scenarios, per model. Each scenario has 32 input variants; failures remain in the denominator. Points are descriptive estimates, without inferential comparisons between strata. The separated neutral-grey symbols show the same three reference strata for the historical ChatGPT Health web product (9–11 January 2026): 318/480 (66.25%), 780/960 (81.25%) and 462/480 (96.25%), respectively. Symbol shapes identify strata for both sources; the four current API models were evaluated in September 2026. Historical values use original author-coded grades rescored against the current acceptable sets after input matching. This post hoc, noncontemporaneous reference is not a fifth concurrent arm and is excluded from the prespecified tests in panel b. Clinical bodies and labels match, but product packaging, full prompts and output coding differ; numerical differences cannot be attributed solely to the models. The historical ambiguous-case value differs from the paper’s 96.0% text; this unresolved discrepancy and matching audit are detailed in Supplementary Figure S8 and Supplementary Table S1. **b,** JEV minus Luna agreement in clear-label inputs in each round. Points and bars are scenario-paired differences and existing 95% percentile bootstrap intervals, respectively, resampling the 15 entire base scenarios 5,000 times with seed 20260922. Round 1 (asterisk) is the prespecified primary analysis; rounds 2 and 3 assess repeatability on the same inputs. Differences are 10.83 [2.92, 19.79], 10.42 [2.29, 19.58] and 9.17 [1.04, 17.92] percentage points. The two first-round secondary comparisons, JEV versus Gemini and versus DeepSeek, each have Holm-adjusted two-sided scenario-paired sign-flip P = 0.085938; neither is significant at 0.05. **c,** Undertriage, agreement, overtriage and technical failure among all 960 planned first-round main requests per model. **d,** Mutually exclusive outcomes across three rounds for each of 960 main inputs: identical and acceptable; identical and incorrect; changed but acceptable in every round; changed with at least one incorrect round; or unassessable because at least one round failed. Counts are shown in the two identical-answer segments; all counts are provided in source data. Superscript 1 denotes identical-answer rates conditional on three valid answers: JEV 886/946, Luna 817/960, Gemini 865/960 and DeepSeek 651/959. Repeated failures are never counted as agreement. For JEV the five categories contain 741, 145, 33, 27 and 14 inputs. Consistency alone does not establish correctness or safety.

## Figure 2 | Probability reliability and measured service characteristics

**a,** Exploratory JEV first-round selective disagreement risk versus coverage in clear-label inputs, using the selected grade's probability. The principal coverage denominator is 476 valid probability-bearing inputs from 480 planned inputs; the source data also provide planned-input coverage. Thresholding uses probability ≥ τ, retaining equal-probability inputs together. The staircase joins attainable selection sets; ties cannot be partially selected. At τ = 0.9, 152 inputs are retained and 8 disagree with their references (5.26%; coverage 31.93% of valid inputs or 31.67% of planned inputs). Thresholds 0.5, 0.7, 0.8 and 0.9 are marked descriptively. Empty selection has undefined risk; no zero-risk empty point is plotted. No deployment threshold is chosen, and selected inputs can be clustered within a few scenarios. **b,** Reliability of selected-grade probabilities in the same 476 inputs using ten fixed equal-width bins over [0,1], including 1 in the final bin. Points show mean probability and observed agreement; labels show bin sizes and the dashed diagonal indicates perfect reliability. Empty bins are omitted without interpolation. The displayed axis is cropped below 0.25, where no inputs occur. Multiclass Brier score is 0.370362 (sum of four squared class-probability errors, averaged over inputs). NLL is infinite because 10 inputs assign zero probability to the reference label; zeros are not clipped. This does not demonstrate superior calibration to other models. **c,** HTTP p50 latency and known cost per 1,000 planned requests at concurrency ceiling 1. Small symbols represent three complete time blocks for each model, each with 200 formal requests; large symbols give coordinate-wise medians over those blocks. Both metrics come from those same measured blocks; the median coordinates need not represent one particular block. Warmups are excluded. Latency uses valid outputs and excludes admission waiting; costs include known charges for failed requests. Hollow DeepSeek diamonds denote peak uncached upper cost estimates, whereas other costs are provider-reported. These 12 cells contain one JEV request with an unknown charge; its affected block reports known cost only. The efficiency experiment contains this one unknown-cost request and the project contains two; neither unknown charge is imputed as zero. The plot describes the tested services, without a dominance frontier, controlled hardware inference or clinical cost-effectiveness claim.

## Table 1 | Benchmark agreement, consistency, undertriage and single-concurrency service profile

Clear-label agreement uses first-round correct/480 planned requests per model. Three-round consistency uses identical decisions / inputs valid in all three rounds, from 960 planned main inputs; it includes consistently incorrect answers. First-round undertriage uses valid main outputs as denominator. HTTP p50 and known US dollars per 1,000 planned requests are medians of three concurrency-1 cells (200 formal requests per cell), with warmups excluded. DeepSeek costs are upper estimates; the remaining costs are provider-reported. The JEV concurrency-1 subset contains one request with an unknown charge; all displayed JEV costs summarize known charges only. Other models have no unknown-cost requests in this subset. Neither permanent budget reservations nor cost per correct output is included in this table.

## Supplementary Figure S3 | Paired objective and subjective information versions

Objective-minus-subjective agreement differences in percentage points for each model and round, using all complete pairs (a) or complete pairs after exclusion of previously flagged inputs (b). Each version is scored against its own reference set. Baseline matching plans 480 pairs per model and round; sensitivity selection removes 144 pairs, leaving 336 before technical failures. Pairs enter the analysis only when both selected endpoints have valid outputs. Losses due to selection and failures, and the resulting valid-pair denominators, are separately recorded in source data. Points are descriptive paired differences, without causal attribution to information availability or multiplicity-unadjusted claims of superiority.

## Supplementary Figure S4 | Single-factor wording perturbations across rounds

Rows of panels represent rounds 1–3; columns represent changed decisions, changes toward lower urgency and changes from an acceptable to an incorrect decision. Heatmap rows represent anchoring, access barriers, gender wording and explicit Black wording; columns follow the fixed model order. Values are percentages of valid matched pairs (480 planned pairs for each factor, model and round before failures). Correct-to-incorrect percentages use all valid pairs, not only pairs initially answered correctly. Matching holds case identity, information version and all nontarget factors constant; `variant_code`, which encodes the combined factors, is excluded from the matching key. Nonunique matches are not allowed. Anchoring and barrier subtypes are aggregated from the frozen matched comparisons. The race wording comparison is explicit Black versus race unspecified; it does not measure differences between real populations. All panels use the same 0–35% colour scale and print the numerical percentages. These are exploratory descriptions, not independent-case or fairness significance tests.

Paired differences in reference agreement between explanation-JSON outputs and structured outputs for clear-label (a; 30 inputs, 15 scenarios) and all main cases (b; 60 inputs, 30 scenarios). There is one explanation sample per model, compared with each of the three existing baseline rounds on matching inputs. ## Supplementary Figure S7 | Service performance across concurrency ceilings

HTTP latency p50 (a), p95 (b), valid-output throughput (c), peak simultaneous HTTP requests (d), admission-wait p50 (e) and technical failure percentage (f) across concurrency ceilings 1, 4, 8, 16 and 32. Each model–ceiling combination contains three complete time-block cells, with 200 formal requests each: 60 cells and 12,000 formal requests in total. All 300 warmups are excluded. Small points show cells, lines show medians and shading shows observed minimum–maximum ranges, not confidence intervals. HTTP percentiles use valid outputs; failures retain the planned-request denominator; effective throughput uses valid outputs over the complete cell measurement interval. JEV's frozen minimum admission interval is 0.06 s (approximately 16.67 admitted requests/s), and nominal concurrency is not equivalent to realized in-flight requests. The observed plateau therefore cannot identify JEV's unthrottled hardware or model throughput. No interrupted-cell recovery interval is treated as a complete measurement.

## Supplementary Figure S8 | Noncontemporaneous ChatGPT Health historical reference

Reference-label agreement for ChatGPT Health collected through the web product on 9–11 January 2026 (a, neutral grey) and the current API models' first round in September 2026 (b, model symbols). Historical and current error directions among 960 main inputs are shown separately (c,d), using common category colours. Historical rows were matched by case identity, information version and factor combination, then checked for normalized clinical body and reference-label equality. All 960 main inputs from 30 scenarios and 128 emergency-supplement inputs from 4 scenarios matched; no inputs were lost. Matching removes the historical output-format instructions, so it does not establish full-prompt identity. Original and Expanded overlap on 960 main rows, which are counted once; 160 additional psychological-extension rows are excluded. Historical grades use the authors' `llm_triage` coding with `response_raw` retained, without automatic recoding or new clinical adjudication. Under the current frozen acceptable-set scoring, historical agreement is 318/480 (66.25%) in clear-label inputs, 462/480 (96.25%) in ambiguous inputs and 780/960 (81.25%) overall; emergency-supplement agreement is 128/128. The original explicit-D result, 33/64 undertriaged, is reproduced. The paper's ambiguous-case text reports 96.0%, whereas current public data yield 96.25% by set membership and 99.375% by a minimum-grade-only criterion. This discrepancy remains unresolved and is not silently reconciled. The historical group is not a concurrent fifth arm or independent external validation; no cross-period significance test, repeated-answer stability, API-cost, latency or JEV-style probability-calibration comparison is performed.
